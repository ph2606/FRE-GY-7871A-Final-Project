# Provider protocol relevance refinement,15October2026

Before expanded retrieval and before comparing provider outcome values, independent title-only review identified irrelevant near-date content in the first538-title candidate sample: physical or wholesale inflation, PPI, generic AI job forecasts, policy speeches without a current release, previews, and multitopic/market roundups whose negative words can concern other subjects. The first-query candidates, selected538 titles, raw responses and acquisition audit are preserved under `data/raw/provider_bias/initial_query_*`.

The Employment Situation query is broadened identically for all nine providers by adding the neutral terms jobs, hiring and employment. The initial query contained payroll, unemployment and report phrases, which can miss simple direct titles such as 'US employers add172,000 jobs'. This expands retrieval without selecting positive/negative terms or particular providers. CPI queries are unchanged. No provider sentiment estimates or forecast outcomes are used to choose query terms or eligibility.

The primary sample now requires a specific current release anchor, not merely a matching topic/date. Employment titles name the jobs/employment report, payroll data, a numerical aggregate jobs/unemployment result, or release-associated official labor revisions. CPI titles name CPI, consumer prices, inflation report/data/reading/breakdown, the official observation month, or a direct current-result inflation headline. Previews and future forecasts are excluded. Primary titles also exclude asset-price reaction headlines and mixed-topic programs/roundups, because the classifier could otherwise label unrelated market losses or other stories instead of wording about the economic release. The broader sample is retained as secondary release-window coverage. Independent AI title-only relevance decisions, including uncertain/exclude labels, are preserved and applied conservatively. This is a transparent retrospective protocol refinement and development screening, not preregistration or untouched validation.

The initial refinement recomputed main common-provider support using only primary eligible titles, without using their model labels, while retaining the original six-brand mandatory core and six-event threshold. That version proved infeasible and is superseded by the coverage-only feasibility amendment below. In the final design, all displayed providers share exactly the same events, and providers outside adequate common support receive no main bar. No claim of perfect same-article/event identity follows from title-only matching.

Observation-period metadata was corrected after a unit test and independent audit: each January release describes December of the prior year, each February release January, etc. The source calendar dates and search windows were correct; all supplemental metadata tables are regenerated from cached responses. Primary confidence limits remain unaltered bootstrap percentile bounds; the chart must draw interval lines independently if a discrete bootstrap interval does not contain its point estimate.


After applying the independent title-only review and checking direct-release evidence, the initially proposed six-brand core had only four common events and therefore failed the unchanged six-event threshold. That initial design is reported as infeasible. The final chart instead requires the two providers explicitly requested (CNBC and Fox News), then mechanically chooses the largest additional-provider panel with at least six identical events; ties follow the original provider order. This coverage-only feasibility amendment was made before computing the final primary provider estimates, without using classifier outcomes to choose brands or events. It retains the strict relevance screen and the six-event threshold. The resulting six-provider panel includes CNBC, Fox News, Fox Business, CNN, Bloomberg and Wall Street Journal; AP, Reuters and NPR receive coverage rows but no comparable main bar. This is a retrospective exploratory comparison, not an untouched or preregistered test. The peer reference is conditional on this selected panel.

An independent AI reviewer judged the original 538 title/date records without classifier outputs. The expanded query added 18 current records, which were reviewed by the primary agent. A confirmed direct-result judgment can resolve the narrow regular-expression missing-anchor condition; it cannot override previews, market reactions, other metrics or multi-topic exclusions. Four conservative relevance adjudications remove political/policy reactions and mixed-topic commentary. Detailed decisions are local at data/annotations/provider_relevance_review.csv and are AI, not human validation. Fresh source-only reproduction can regenerate the automated corpus, but exactly reproducing this relevance refinement requires the saved local review or a new documented blind review.


The final implementation also standardizes administrative title formatting before scoring: it removes a leading Watch prefix and a trailing publisher-brand suffix such as | CNN Business or - WSJ Podcasts. Raw title strings are retained unchanged. Exact normalized canonical-title duplicates within provider/event are excluded from the primary calculation without replacement. This prevents administrative branding and repeated text across article/video formats from defining the framing contrast. It does not remove actors or substantive economic language. The scoring-protocol tag is saved and checked before cached probabilities are reused; incompatible old scores are recomputed. This preprocessing amendment is disclosed as development refinement, not classifier validation.
