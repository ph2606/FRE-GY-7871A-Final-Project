# Association now, stronger identification next

This project selects the assignment's **observational analysis with a causal-design proposal** option. No participants were recruited and no experiment was conducted. The design below is a prospective protocol, not an empirical result.

## What the available study can estimate

Let `Y_t` be Michigan sentiment for month `t`, `X_t` the macroeconomic information actually released before the specified forecast origin, `M_t` a source-balanced media measure available by that origin, and `H_t` household or political controls. Compare economic-only, economic-plus-media and full-control predictions chronologically. A sentiment gap is observed `Y_t` minus the economic-only out-of-sample prediction. It is model error and omitted information as well as possible perceptions.

A negative coefficient on lagged tone is an association. Economic shocks affect both media and household assessments; consumers choose information and publishers respond to demand; partisan affiliation can influence exposure and answers. Measurement error and source composition are additional causes of apparent relationships. Including controls cannot prove all these paths have been blocked. Granger predictability, if tested with enough observations, is incremental temporal prediction rather than a causal media effect.

For textual selection, compare the **same release/event** across a predetermined set of national and local outlets. Use a release register independent of corpus tone, common headline units, publication windows, source fixed effects and event fixed effects. Separate worsening real outcomes from emotional amplification and price-level framing. Source/event controls do not repair ranked/capped retrieval; report inclusion fractions and coverage failure. Equivalent positive/negative surprise tests require an independently archived pre-release consensus. If that consensus is unavailable, do not label realized changes “surprises.”

## Proposed truthful-framing experiment

**Target and assignment.** After required institutional review, recruit consenting U.S. adults from a probability-based or carefully benchmarked panel. Record baseline economic beliefs, household burdens, news habits and party affiliation before treatment. Randomize individuals within prespecified income, age and affiliation blocks to one of three summaries of the **same already published official facts**:

1. Balanced fact sheet showing annual inflation, cumulative price change, unemployment and the latest released GDP, with units and release dates.
2. The same fact sheet with a headline emphasizing the cumulative price level.
3. The same fact sheet with a headline emphasizing the annual inflation rate.

Numbers, uncertainty, source links, length and reading difficulty remain common. The contrasting headline emphasis must be truthful. Use no fabricated stories or partisan endorsements. Archive the exact stimuli before recruitment. The primary contrast is arm 2 minus arm 3; the balanced arm assesses each relative to a common presentation. This isolates a defined framing manipulation; it does not represent all negative economic coverage.

**Outcomes and timing.** Prespecify a primary next-day sentiment-related composite with identical baseline and follow-up wording. Retain its components separately: household finances, business outlook and durable-buying conditions. Secondary outcomes are numerical beliefs about prices/unemployment/GDP, recall of the factual summary, confidence in beliefs and intended spending. Collect immediate and one-week follow-ups to assess persistence. An adapted survey response is not the official Michigan index. Debrief respondents and provide the balanced fact sheet.

**Analysis.** Estimate intention-to-treat mean differences, adjusting for baseline outcome and randomization blocks, with robust uncertainty. Analyze assignment regardless of reading/comprehension; do not restrict the primary effect to compliant readers. Report allocation, attrition and missingness by arm. Prespecify handling of missing outcomes and use attrition sensitivity/bounds where defensible. Select sample size from a declared minimally relevant effect and precision target before collecting data. Register the primary contrast, outcome, follow-up, exclusion rules and multiplicity adjustment. Apply Holm correction to the finite family of secondary contrasts; clearly label exploratory subgroup interactions.

**Mechanisms and moderators.** A shift in recall and beliefs alongside the randomized outcome is evidence consistent with a framing channel; it does not automatically identify causal mediation. Test affiliation, income and baseline price concern as prespecified moderators rather than substitute definitions of bias. Measure prior price anchors explicitly if testing anchoring. Test confirmation bias only with randomized information that interacts with baseline beliefs and measured attention/acceptance. A content classifier alone measures neither cognitive mechanism.

**Assumptions and checks.** Randomization must be implemented independently of outcomes. Participants should receive only their assigned summary; avoid household cross-arm contamination and measure possible sharing. Check balance descriptively, document failures and outcome-blind corrections, and retain the registered primary analysis. Limit demand cues by describing the task as economic-information comprehension rather than an attempt to change pessimism. Expect survey participation and experimental exposure to differ from habitual browsing; this limits external validity even when internal validity is sound.

## Linking any future effect to national sentiment

A national effect requires more than multiplying a convenience-sample effect by a media item count. Estimate population exposure to the defined framing (ideally consented browser/app or media-diary data), compare panel coverage and response to the Michigan target population, and reweight on relevant observed moderators with adequate overlap. Actual exposure differs from content supply, likes, reposts and self-reported platform use. Account for repeated exposure, displacement of other information and interference through discussion.

Even with weighting, transportability requires that relevant effect modifiers are measured and that the intervention resembles natural exposure. Clearly separate the experimental effect on a study response from a counterfactual change in the published national index. Michigan's May 2024 report describes multiple information sources, including direct experience and interpersonal discussion; that motivates measuring these competing channels. [University of Michigan, May 3, 2024](https://www.sca.isr.umich.edu/files/newssources2024.pdf).

## Falsification and financial use

Evidence against the proposed media explanation includes: no stable matched-event tone asymmetry; poor human agreement or weak source-specific classifier performance; no same-holdout forecast gain; results disappearing under price-level, household burden or source-composition controls; a gap that changes materially with real-time data vintages or survey-mode treatment; or randomized framing failing to change beliefs and outcomes with useful precision. A null is informative only when measurement and uncertainty permit meaningful effects to be excluded.

Until these checks pass, use the monitor as a diagnostic input for consumer-demand scenarios. Escalate a persistent negative residual for review alongside real income, cumulative prices and financing conditions; require independent validation before changing forecast weights or positions. No causal national impact or profitable strategy is established by this proposed design.
