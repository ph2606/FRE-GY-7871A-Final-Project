# Part B: evidence ledger and interpretation

Reviewed October 18, 2026. Part B is integrated into the main report at the user's explicit request to disregard the original 10-12 page limit. The presentation retains the assignment's 6-8 minute target. Part A's cutoff remains October 13; the separate reader/demography extension must not be mistaken for information used in the original forecasts.

The manifesto's thesis is normative: independent judgment and opportunities to practice it deserve protection. Its proposed mechanism is reduced practice, attention to accuracy and access to usable evidence, not an established decline in global IQ or a demographic hierarchy. Part A contains no individual cognitive tests. The literature review is targeted, not a preregistered systematic review; publication and selection bias remain possible.

| Question | Primary evidence | Inference allowed; boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Is IQ falling everywhere? | Pietschnig & Voracek, *One Century of Global IQ Gains*, [2015](https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691615577701); Bratsberg & Rogeberg, *Flynn effect and its reversal*, [2018](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1718793115) | Historical gains and setting-specific reversals coexist. Norwegian within-family patterns support an environmental interpretation for those male cohorts. Neither study supplies a current representative global annual IQ series. |
| Is there relevant genetic/fertility evidence? | Kong et al., *Selection against variants associated with educational attainment*, [2017](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1612113114) | An Icelandic association between an education-related polygenic score and reproduction is narrower than an IQ forecast. Do not infer genes, intelligence or reproductive motives from country income or family size. |
| Could scarcity affect measured performance? | Mani et al., *Poverty impedes cognitive function*, [2013](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1238041) | Financial concerns and changing conditions within people can affect tasks; context-specific evidence does not classify fixed ability or estimate every poverty effect. This is one study, not a systematic estimate of all replications. |
| Does AI weaken learning? | Bastani et al., *Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning*, [2025](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2422633122), corrected publication Table 1 | Classroom randomization supports a short-term learning effect in that school. Hint-based design changes the result. Permanent IQ loss and world extrapolations are unsupported. Our chart uses summary coefficients and approximate normal intervals, not copied graphics. |
| Does faster social media mean a shorter human attention span? | Lorenz-Spreen et al., *Accelerating dynamics of collective attention*, [2019](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-019-09311-w) | Topic turnover is a collective attention measure. It is not an individual cognitive test, a causal estimate of platform harm, or a measure of genetic change. |
| What about individual attention and short videos? | Nguyen et al., *Feeds, feelings, and focus*, [2025](https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000498); original abstract verified through [NCBI XML](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pubmed&id=41231585&retmode=xml) | Review-wide totals are 71 studies and 98,299 participants across cognitive and mental-health outcomes. Attention association r=-.38 is a correlation, not a 38% decrease, a causal effect, an attention-only sample total or a change in IQ. Reverse causation and selection remain relevant. |
| Can readers improve? | Pennycook & Rand, *Accuracy prompts are replicable and generalizable*, [2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-30073-5) | Experiments support improvements in sharing discernment under those conditions. Sharing intentions differ from observed lifetime behavior; results challenge claims of universal inability. |
| How do birth patterns compare? | [World Bank fertility](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/SP.DYN.TFRT.IN) and [crude birth rate](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/SP.DYN.CBRT.IN), frozen WDI snapshot | Two different population metrics, 1990-2023. They contain no IQ measurements. Selected countries are illustrative; income aggregates use the grouping in the download. |
| Do people consider finances before children? | UNFPA, *The Real Fertility Crisis*, [2025](https://www.unfpa.org/swp2025) | Reported constraints across a 14-country survey are evidence about aspirations and barriers, not an intelligence test or all-world census. |
| Is this backward evolution? | UC Berkeley Understanding Evolution, [Trends in Evolution](https://evolution.berkeley.edu/evolution-101/the-big-issues/trends-in-evolution/) | Evolution has no universal direction defined by human values. Social or educational deterioration is conceptually different from a demonstrated genetic trend. |

## What would change the manifesto?

Test source/date prompts on independently verified economic claims, with delayed unaided accuracy on previously unseen claims as the primary outcome. Register the sample, outcome, contrast, missingness and multiplicity treatment before recruitment. A well-powered null, loss of gains on new claims, harmful overconfidence, or durable benefits from ordinary AI use would require revision. No such experiment was performed here. Wider financial or social consequences are hypotheses requiring their own evidence, not estimates inherited from Part A.
