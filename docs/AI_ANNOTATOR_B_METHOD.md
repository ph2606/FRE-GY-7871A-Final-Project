# Independent AI annotation B: diagnostic only

On 13 October 2026 the B assistant read the frozen media codebook and the independently shuffled B worksheet, then judged all 300 displayed titles individually. It did not inspect A's labels, the sampling key, individual contextual-model probabilities, or dictionary outputs. The assistant had previously worked on aggregate macro/media forecasting results; that prior aggregate knowledge is an unavoidable qualification to its blinding.

Tone, emotional wording, eight salience flags, six framing flags, political actor/attribution, numerical-claim eligibility, confidence, a supporting excerpt and notes are completed. Labels concern the displayed wording, not unobserved article content or readers. Negative factual outcomes may receive negative tone with emotional wording marked no. Mixed labels preserve opposing signals rather than forcing them into one polarity. Numerical eligibility is not factual verification. Confidence uses 0.95 for clear judgments, 0.75 for meaningful ambiguity and 0.50 for substantial ambiguity; these are subjective confidence codes, not calibrated probabilities.

All 300 original rows are preserved, including word-sense errors, navigation pages and foreign-only items. Clear irrelevant items are identified in notes; a later eligibility screen can restrict agreement estimates without silently replacing the review pool. Frame/salience presence refers only to visible title evidence. Political mentions are distinguished from explicit causal credit or blame; attributed claims do not imply endorsement.

The assistant hand-entered a row-by-row judgment map. A local transcription script only expands these selected codes into worksheet columns and checks completeness, identifier uniqueness, unchanged input ordering and exact supporting excerpts. It contains no dictionary, classifier, automated sentiment rule or model-generated batch-labeling routine. Each supporting excerpt is a contiguous case-insensitive substring of its title. The output is `data/annotations/ai_annotator_B.csv`; the local entry map remains with the ignored annotation data.

These are AI judgments. They are neither human annotation nor an independent human gold standard. Agreement between AI A and AI B can reveal measurement disagreements and guide later review; it does not satisfy the assignment's requirement for two independent human annotators or establish classifier accuracy.
