# Frozen media measurement codebook

Version 1, frozen before first project scoring, 13 October 2026. This is an operational study protocol, not a claim that the labels have been validated by humans.

## Population, sample and benchmark

The target is publicly indexed US economic content, not media consumption by Michigan respondents. Fixed national outlets: Associated Press (apnews.com), Reuters (reuters.com), NPR (npr.org). Fixed local outlets: Gothamist (New York metro), Texas Tribune (Texas), Colorado Sun (Colorado). These geographically diverse local outlets are a convenience panel, not a representative US geography sample. No political-balance classification is assigned. The social stratum is Hacker News story titles, a technology-oriented convenience platform, not representative US adults. Audience sizes and survey respondent exposure are unavailable. National versus local describes the publisher rather than every item's subject.

Monthly Google News RSS searches use each fixed domain and the same positive/negative-neutral term family: economy, inflation, unemployment, cost of living, GDP, wages, housing. Algolia searches economy, inflation, unemployment, wages, housing, and cost of living separately, with exact non-prefix matching and title-level relevance filtering. Search/index visibility and retrospective ranking determine the candidate pool. At most 100 RSS results can be returned per query; caps are audited rather than treated as exhaustive counts. Algolia date ranges are recursively split if its 1,000-hit window is reached. Candidates are sampled by a deterministic hash within each outlet-month, maximum 12 unique titles, independent of sentiment and engagement. Missing outlet-months remain missing. The monthly index first averages titles within each outlet, then outlets within national/local/social, then available group means; an all-three-groups version and coverage flag are retained. Source counts, group coverage and cap rates accompany every index. Composition changes can change a mean.

Dates run from January 2020 to 12 October 2026 inclusive in New York; October is incomplete. All records retain publisher publication time, retrieval time, request URL and response SHA-256. A publication timestamp is a retrospective availability proxy, not proof that a historical index contained the story then. News observations are headlines, and social observations are user-posted story titles; no full articles, comments or implied publisher endorsement are scored. Empty strings, out-of-window records, titles without economic terms, clearly foreign-only titles, duplicates and obvious advertising titles are excluded with reason codes. Unknown geographic relevance is retained and counted. Repeated normalized headlines and story IDs are deduplicated; near duplicates, coordinated promotion and bot identity cannot be reliably detected. HN usernames are hashed, activity bursts are flagged descriptively, and engagement counts are retrieval-vintage attention proxies excluded from primary weights.

Negative wording is not evidence of bias. Selection is benchmarked only within the captured, identically queried source panel and shared event clusters, not against the entire news universe. A complete denominator of all published coverage is unavailable, so topic proportions mean conditional salience among sampled economic titles. News coverage of objectively bad outcomes can legitimately be negative. Equivalent surprise comparisons require contemporaneous expectations, which are not observed here.

## Dimensions and permissible labels

1. **Tone:** negative / neutral / positive / mixed / cannot determine. Judge the economic wording in the displayed title. Separate factual adverse outcomes from emotional wording. A factual layoff report may be negative in valence without emotionally loaded language. Human annotators additionally mark emotional wording yes/no/unclear and copy the shortest supporting excerpt. CardiffNLP contextual probabilities are uncalibrated, with tone P(positive) minus P(negative), range [-1,1], class argmax. VADER provides a fixed dictionary baseline with thresholds -0.05 and 0.05. Neither classifier directly separates factual from emotional negativity; the latter has a transparent keyword flag and awaits human validation.
2. **Selection/salience:** independently mark layoffs, hiring, rising prices, falling inflation, GDP/growth, recession, housing, wages, other economic. Multiple labels allowed. Rules match literal concepts and directional price phrases; unmatched is not absent from a full article. Shares are conditional title attention. Benchmark same queries and outlet mix, same release/event periods; do not equate attention with distortion.
3. **Framing:** price levels, inflation rates, household experience, national aggregate, current conditions, forecast. Each is present/absent/unclear and may co-occur. Rule outputs are literal proxies, not learned validated frame labels. Elevated price levels and falling inflation can both be true. Price-level minus inflation-rate share is a framing contrast rather than a bias score.
4. **Factual calibration:** first identify an explicit numerical economic claim, then metric definition, observation period, release/vintage available at publication, units, source value and uncertainty. Human result: supported / contradicted / misleading definition or timing / unverifiable / no checkable claim. A numeric token is only screening eligibility; it is never factual error. Headlines alone often lack the definition or period. No factual-accuracy rate is reported without completed official-vintage checks. The calibration worksheet and an official-release matching aid are supplied; automated values remain unverified. Official references: https://www.bls.gov/bls/news-release/empsit.htm ; https://www.bea.gov/data/personal-consumption-expenditures-price-index ; https://www.bea.gov/data/gdp/gross-domestic-product ; https://www.sca.isr.umich.edu/ .
5. **Political attribution:** actor/institution mention and explicit credit/blame separately. Permissible actors: president/administration, Democrats, Republicans, Federal Reserve, Congress, other, none; valence credit/blame/mixed/none/unclear. Rule actor mention is not attribution; a directional attribution proxy requires a credit/blame verb in the same title. Criticism is not coded as bias. Attribution to an actor is different from author endorsement.

## AI specification and perturbations

No generative LLM is used and no prompt is involved in inference. Model: cardiffnlp/twitter-roberta-base-sentiment-latest, immutable revision 3216a57f2a0d9c45a2e6c20157c20c49fb4bf9c7. Tokenizer/config/checkpoint and official metadata are copied into this project and hashed; no remote code. Full titles are tokenized and any rare long title is split into nonoverlapping 510-token payload chunks with token-count weighted class probabilities. Sources and political labels are not appended to the primary input. Hyperparameters and rules are frozen before project outcome analysis. Model was trained on Twitter text through 2021 and introduced in 2022, so 2020–2021 contextual scores are retrospective measurements using later language representations. Retrospective Google ranking and retrieval-vintage HN metadata likewise prevent a genuine historical deployment backtest. Post-2022 and dictionary sensitivities should be considered; predictions are retrospective pseudo-out-of-sample associations.

Diagnostics replace Trump ↔ Biden, Republican ↔ Democrat and prepend otherwise identical titles with Reuters versus Gothamist. Report tone deltas/class switches; changed names can change semantics, so this is a robustness stress test and not a proof of discrimination. Frozen title inputs and perturbation inputs/scores are preserved. Model sources: https://huggingface.co/cardiffnlp/twitter-roberta-base-sentiment-latest/blob/3216a57f2a0d9c45a2e6c20157c20c49fb4bf9c7/README.md and https://aclanthology.org/2022.acl-demo.25/ . HN API: https://hn.algolia.com/api . VADER: https://github.com/cjhutto/vaderSentiment .

## Independent annotation protocol

The same stratified sample of at least 300 unique titles is sent to two different people in independently shuffled order. Sampling covers each source, calendar regime (2020–21, 2022–23, 2024–26), preliminary AI class, and claim-eligibility category where available. The two worksheets hide source names, dates, AI outputs, engagement and each other's judgments. Titles themselves may reveal actors or places; perfect blinding is impossible. Annotators must complete their own file independently before seeing adjudication. Do not use an LLM as either human annotator. No consent or study participation is implied by these blank worksheets; this is document annotation of public titles, not a survey experiment.

Complete tone, emotional wording, all salience/frame booleans, political actor/attribution, claim eligibility, confidence and evidence. Allowed uncertain labels are explicit. Adjudicate disagreements only after the independent files are locked; record third judgment and reason, retaining both original judgments. Factual calibration is a separate unblinded pass with source/date, official release links and observation periods because temporal verification cannot be blinded to dates. Do not infer a claim's period or definition when absent.

`src/validate_annotations.py` accepts completed independent worksheets and optional adjudication. It refuses empty or invalid tone labels, computes raw agreement and Cohen's kappa for tone, per-dimension agreement where completed, adjudication-based accuracy/macro-F1, class precision/recall/F1, and source-specific performance for contextual versus VADER. It writes status pending if valid labels are missing. Human validation is a required outstanding deliverable until two real annotators complete it; model-versus-dictionary agreement is diagnostic agreement, not accuracy.

## Interpretation

Text can measure literal framing and content selection. Confirmation bias, availability bias, negativity bias in readers, and media effects on national sentiment require exposure, recall/belief measures or experiments. Price-level framing is compatible with rational household budget concern. Failure of incremental forecast improvement, instability under source/rule choices, successful household-control models, or calibrated negative factual coverage would contradict a simple media-bias account. No causal claims follow from this corpus.

## Declared development amendments

The final relevance audit and reproducibility amendments are recorded in `MEDIA_PROTOCOL_AMENDMENTS.md`. A conservative post-sampling screen removed explicit physical-inflation, entertainment/proper-name false positives, unrelated single political/sport/court jobs, and additional clearly foreign titles without US context. Removed titles were not replaced. Ambiguous geographic names (Georgia, Jordan) and US territory Puerto Rico were protected. The original300 independent annotation pool, its model-score snapshot and source key remain frozen, with `eligible_after_final_screen` flags. Evaluation on this inspected pool is development diagnostic evidence, not an untouched validation holdout. Actual sampling was source × calendar era × preliminary contextual class; numeric-claim eligibility is audited within the resulting sample rather than being a separate stratification dimension. Algolia reports non-exhaustive search estimates for some queries; recall beyond returned/indexed hits remains uncertain even when reported hit windows are covered.

After the user requested completion without human input, two independent AI annotators were used for an explicitly labeled diagnostic of the same300 titles. AI annotation is not human labeling and does not satisfy the two-human-annotator requirement. Blank human worksheets and metrics remain available. A separate purposive eight-title unemployment numeric-alignment check was completed against archived official BLS releases; it does not estimate population factual accuracy and does not verify all title definitions or intraday vintages.
